# Output & pricing review — July 2026

*Are the case studies and reports polished and valuable enough to command real money?
An audit of our own output, benchmarked against brand-rating agencies, research-data
subscriptions, premium editorial subscriptions, and the African competitive set.*

---

## The reframe

**We are not a publication with a paywall. We are the only rating agency for African
brand-culture *craft* — and the market already pays well for a worse version of what we do.**

The competitive map, verified:

| Who | What they actually rate |
|---|---|
| Brand Finance (Africa 200 / SA 100) | Brand **financial value**. Publishes 0–100 BSI + AAA+/D letter ratings. |
| Brand Africa 100 | Brand **admiration** (survey, GeoPoll fieldwork, ISO 20252-aligned). |
| WARC / MMA SMARTIES | **Awards won** — derivative of jury decisions. |
| Culture Hive, Relevance Index, Brandgeist, CIIM | Cultural relevance — **all US-only**. |
| **MonoKromatik** | **The work itself. Nobody else does this.** |

And the price anchor is already established by awards — which deliver a verdict
*once a year, by a jury, with no reasoning attached*:

| Validation the market already buys | Price |
|---|---|
| Loeries 2026 entry | R3,850 – R7,815 **per entry** |
| Effie SA 2026 entry | R9,464 – R19,552 **per entry** |
| A single agency's awards season | **R50k – R500k+** |

> A reasoned, evidenced, year-round rating is a *better product* than an annual jury verdict —
> priced against a proven anchor.

**The gap, stated plainly (from the competitive research): "The space between R50/mo
journalism and $6,000 league tables is empty."** That space is ours.

## Part 1 — What the audit found in our own output

### 🔴 Five paid pieces are indefensible
Premium case studies overall are good — **median 1,712 words, 8 sources**. But five of the
26 people pay for are not:

| Paid piece | Body prose | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Achraf Hakimi and the Morocco Model | 255w | 2 |
| Kava: Nollywood Builds the Rails | 257w | 2 |
| Tailoring Black Style: The Met Gala | 260w | 2 |
| The Black Book | 266w | 2 |
| Tebogo, De Beers and Botswana | 290w | 2 |

A subscriber who pays R149, clicks "premium decode," and gets 255 words with two sources
cancels — and tells people. **Highest-risk item on the site.**

### 🔴 17 of 28 reports contain no chart at all
Only 11 carry an exhibit. Every benchmark said the same thing, in different words:

> *"The paid artefact is not prose — it's a decision object. Nobody pays for the essay."*

Buyers pay for (ranked): the comparative graphic they can screenshot into a deck; structured
data they can re-cut; analyst time; redistribution rights. **Length is not on the list.**

### 🟡 The free tier is a weak shop window
Free case studies run a **313-word median**. That's the browsing experience that must
convince someone to pay — currently it argues against paying.

### 🟡 Methodology page: good posture, one gap
Live, names the analyst, explains the weights, has a corrections-and-integrity policy,
says "the scores are open, cite them." Strong. **But zero worked examples** — no "here is
how we scored Brand X to 76." For a subjective score, that's the cheapest credibility available.

### 🟢 The machine works now
Depth is bimodal: 39 pieces under 800w (legacy stubs) and 32 at 1,500w+ (recent work).
Recent output is at standard. The archive is dragging the average and the perception.

## Part 2 — What the benchmarks say premium output looks like

**The page-one formula** (CB Insights / Gartner / Forrester, near-identical):
1. Verdict line
2. **The proprietary graphic** (scorecard / quadrant / ranking)
3. 4–5 bulleted "what this means for you"
4. Named analyst byline + date
5. One-line method/sample statement

Body prose is the *justification*, not the product.

**Gate the export, not the read** (Statista): let the rating and the chart be public;
charge for the CSV/PDF, the full scoring breakdown, and reuse rights. Feeds SEO and
citation while still monetising.

**The free tier should be a citation engine** (Stratechery): the free layer exists to be
quoted and linked. At near-zero traffic, citation is our only viable distribution.

**A recurring flagship data artifact buys credibility** (BoF500 / State of Fashion; Brand
Finance Africa 200). One annual, named, downloadable report with published methodology is
how a young outlet becomes the reference.

**Reprint/badge licensing is the margin engine** (Gartner): the *rated party* pays to
republish the badge. Fully available to a one-person operation.

## Part 3 — The uncomfortable truth about the subscription

- **Stears** (Nigeria) raised ~$4m, built a beloved consumer subscription, then **wound the
  consumer division down and pivoted to B2B** — enterprise was already 75% of revenue.
- Median free→paid newsletter conversion is **0.62%**. On ~0 traffic that is ~0 revenue.
- The only proven recurring African subscription price is **Kasi Insight at $350/mo** (B2B data).
  The only proven SA media paywall is **MarkLives at R50/mo**.
- SA consumer ceiling for context: News24 ~110k subs; Daily Maverick ~27.5k members.

**Keep the R149 membership — as credibility and audience proof, not as the business.**

## Part 4 — The plan, prioritised

### Tier 1 — Fix what would embarrass us (days, cheap, high impact)
1. **The five thin paid pieces** — deepen to standard or reclassify to public. Do this first.
2. **An exhibit in every report** — a consistently-styled scorecard/chart. Cut prose if needed.
3. **Standardise report page-one** to the five-part formula above.
4. **Add a worked example** to the methodology page ("how we scored X to 76").

### Tier 2 — Repackage what already exists (weeks)
5. **Make the Index longitudinal** — year-on-year scores are what turn a ranking into a
   licensable dataset. Without repeat measurement there is no data product.
6. **Ship one annual flagship**: *The Cultural-Signal Index 2026* — free ranking, paid depth,
   named methodology, downloadable. This is the BoF500 move; it is what makes us quotable.
7. **Restructure the price ladder** to fill the empty middle:

| Tier | Price | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Membership | R149/mo *(keep)* | Credibility + audience proof, not revenue |
| **Index Access seat** *(new)* | **~R3,500–6,000/yr** | Survives budget review as a *work tool* (The Information Pro logic) |
| **Brand Signal Audit** *(new)* | **R7,500–19,500** | Priced directly against Effie/Loeries entry — a *reasoned* verdict |
| **Reprint / badge licence** *(new)* | **R15,000–40,000** | Gartner's margin engine; the rated party pays |
| Data licence / API | $6k–15k *(keep)* | Anchor high; nobody in this market publishes prices |

8. **Rebuild the R690 team tier** — "nobody buys a team plan for reading." Make it
   Index access + a quarterly data cut + one briefing call.

### Tier 3 — The motion (ongoing)
9. **Sell to the rated, not the readers.** Every brand we score is a prospect: score → badge →
   audit offer. Backlinks, referral traffic and pipeline in one motion.
10. **Events.** Semafor takes >50% of $40m revenue from events. One Joburg/Lagos roundtable
    will out-earn a year of R149 subscriptions and builds the network that generates scoops.

## Part 5 — Verified African pricing (and one correction to the plan above)

Deep-dive on Stears / TechCabal Insights / Asoko / Briter / Africa: The Big Deal. **Four of
five publish no prices at all.** Verified findings:

| Player | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Africa: The Big Deal** | **$219/yr** (deals database, sold via Gumroad) | The ONLY transactable published price in African BI. Free weekly newsletter is the citation engine; the database is the product. *(Corrected 20 Jul 2026 — previously recorded here as $249; the listed price is $219/yr, verified on africathebigdeal.com. The lower anchor matters: it sets the ceiling for a self-serve African BI seat.)* |
| Stears | Not published (quote-only) | **Stears Premium was ~$100/yr — discontinued Oct 2023.** |
| TechCabal Insights | Not published | Reports **free, email-gated**; money is in custom research/consulting. |
| Asoko Insight | Not published | Buyers: LSE, AfDB, UK DBT, US DFC, USAID. |
| Briter | Not published | Public research part-funded by **FMO (verified), Gates Foundation, FCDO**. |

### 🔴 The uncomfortable coincidence
**Our R149/mo is R1,788/yr ≈ $99/yr — almost exactly Stears Premium's ~$100/yr price point.**
Stears had ~$4m and the best consumer product in African business media, and **shut it down**.
We are priced precisely where the strongest-funded attempt concluded there was no business.

### ✅ What this VALIDATES
The proposed **Index Access seat at ~R3,500–6,000/yr** brackets **$219/yr (~R4,000)** — the one
proven self-serve price in this market. Not speculative; anchored.

### ⚠️ Two corrections to Part 4
1. **The annual flagship should be FREE and email-gated, not "free ranking / paid depth."**
   Across TechCabal, Briter and Stears, *reports are universally the funnel, never the product* —
   nobody sells a report per-unit at a listed price. Monetise the **relationship** (audits, seats,
   licences), not the download.
2. **The market is barbelled** — ~$219 self-serve at one end, quote-only enterprise at the other,
   **nothing published in between.** Read this as: sell either at the prosumer anchor OR at
   enterprise quote-only. Don't invent a mid-price nobody in the market has proven.

## Part 6 — African media pricing (and a correction to Part 5)

### ✅ R149/mo is CORRECTLY priced — this corrects the "Stears" alarm above
**The Africa Report charges exactly R149/mo · R1,490/yr in South Africa** (geo-priced;
₦4,900 Nigeria, KES 950 Kenya). We are priced identically to a pan-African premium title
that is a going concern. The ZAR band:

| Publication | ZAR/month |
|---|---|
| Mail & Guardian | R99 |
| **News24** (Africa's largest paid news site) | R109 |
| Business Day Premium | R129 |
| **The Africa Report** | **R149** |
| **MonoKromatik** | **R149** |

So the price is right. **The problem was never the number — it's that consumer news
subscriptions don't scale in this market:** News24 + Netwerk24 sit at 212,473 subscribers,
up just **+1.6% YoY**. Naspers' scale, flatlined.

### 🎯 The single most useful finding in the whole review
> **"The paid product is rarely the news."**

The Africa Report's paywall doesn't gate articles — it gates **rankings (Top 200 Banks,
Top 500 Companies), country reports, and EVENT ACCESS** (incl. Africa CEO Forum priority).
Semafor and Rest of World give the journalism away entirely.

**We already own the equivalent asset: the Index is a ranking.** This validates the whole
direction — gate the Index depth, the data and the access. Never the essays.

### Pay-what-you-can outperforms tiering in SA
**Daily Maverick**: 32,000 Maverick Insiders, **no tiers**, voluntary R75–R350+/mo,
**membership = 40% of total revenue** — the strongest African reader-revenue result
relative to size. They also won back 10,000 lapsed members (the only public African
churn-recovery case study). Worth testing against our fixed R149.

### Also proven
- **Geo-pricing works**: Jeune Afrique's dynamic, per-market pricing + mobile-money options
  **doubled** subscription-offer conversion. Relevant the moment we sell beyond SA.
- **Events, again**: Semafor 2025 — ~$40m revenue, first profitable year, events grew from
  30% (2022) to **>50%**.
- **Nobody can quote a reliable African "% who pay for news"** — Reuters Institute
  deliberately withholds it (online, English-only, 18–50 samples). Treat any such stat as false.

### Strategic warning
**You cannot out-give the donor-funded players.** Briter's and TechCabal's free research is
subsidised by Gates/FMO/FCDO and DFI contracts. Competing on volume-of-free is unwinnable.
The only defensible ground is being **the sole rater of African brand-culture craft.**

## What NOT to do
- **Don't write more articles.** 119 articles produced 0 star performers last week. The 120th
  behaves the same. Distribution and packaging are the constraint, not volume.
- **Don't chase consumer subscription growth** as the primary revenue plan. Stears already
  ran that experiment with $4m behind it.
- **Don't sell sponsored research** without care — independence is the one asset that can't
  be sold twice, and it is the entire basis of a rating.

## Watchlist
**Nielsen is exiting South Africa** (~12-month window, BRC confirmed) — a real vacuum opening
in African measurement credibility.
