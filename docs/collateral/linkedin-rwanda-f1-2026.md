## POST — Rwanda's F1 deal  (personal profile)

Formula 1 is returning to Africa for the first time since 1993 — in Rwanda, not South Africa. Everyone's reading it as a feel-good moment. I think it's a value-capture question in disguise.

Here's the deal: a 10-year agreement, signed in Kigali in October, for a purpose-built circuit near Bugesera. The first race lands in the early 2030s. The symbolism is huge — F1's own president called Africa "the missing piece."

But follow the money, not the flag.

A Grand Prix is a rented platform. Formula 1's rights are owned by Liberty Media, and a host pays an annual fee for a decade of access. That fee leaves the country, every year. What a host gets back splits into two ledgers:

→ What leaves: the hosting fee, plus whatever share of the build is imported.
→ What stays: the circuit and airport (a ~$2bn complex co-funded with Qatar), a tourism engine, a skills base, and the brand of being "the country that brought F1 back to Africa."

The bet works if what stays outruns what leaves. And here's the catch that should bother anyone reading the headlines: the two numbers that decide it — the construction budget and the annual hosting fee — have not been disclosed. You can't price a bet whose two biggest line items are hidden.

There's a shadow over it too: the same global billboard that amplifies Rwanda's best story also amplifies a contested one, with the DRC formally lobbying F1 and sponsors to cut ties. Amplification is indifferent to what it amplifies.
