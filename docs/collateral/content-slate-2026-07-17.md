**1. Ikoyi Chapmans — who owns a communal Nigerian recipe once it's a brand?** · *case study*
On 12 Jul, British-Nigerian promoters + CAADES Group (Lagos) signed a pan-African deal to
commercialise the Chapman — the non-alcoholic citrus-bitters cocktail invented c.1960s at
Lagos's Ikoyi Club — as a packaged RTD, SA retail "within weeks." The IDEA is African and
communal (no single owner); the ownership structure being built around it is the story. Textbook
authorship-vs-capture.
Source: vanguardngr.com/2026/07/strategic-partnership-unveils-ikoyi-chapmans... · *no revenue figures given — don't invent.*

**2. eMedia × Netflix ("The Four of Us") — the broadcaster who kept the IP** · *case study*
~9 Jul: eMedia licenses its daily drama to Netflix (e.tv 8pm → Netflix 9am next day) but
Tshedza Pictures/eMedia **retain ownership** — Netflix buys distribution only. The inverse of the
usual "sell the catalogue to the platform" trade: who owns the screen vs who rents it. Distinct
from our existing "Polygamist tops Netflix" piece (that's reach; this is ownership).
Source: news.broadcastmediaafrica.com/2026/07/09/emedia-partners-with-netflix...

**3. Vodacom takes 55% of Safaricom — a South African giant buys East Africa's crown jewel** · *report (market briefing)*
