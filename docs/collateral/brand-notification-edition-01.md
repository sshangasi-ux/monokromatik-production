## Why we do this

Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It's fair.** We are publishing a rating of a named company's work. Some of those
   ratings are low — three works score AUTHORSHIP 1, meaning we judge that African culture
   was referenced with no African authorship at all. Telling a company that *after* it
   appears in the press is a drive-by. Telling them first, with a route to respond, is a
   rating.
2. **It's already our published policy.** The methodology page states: *"If you are a rated
   brand, you can respond. Send us evidence… New evidence can move a score; that is precisely
   how the Index is meant to work."* This makes good on that rather than inventing anything.
3. **It is the strongest credibility act available to us.** Brand Africa buys legitimacy with
   GeoPoll fieldwork and an integrity advisory board. We cannot. What we *can* do is behave
   like a rating agency: publish the rubric in advance, notify the rated party, take evidence,
   and publish corrections. That behaviour is free and it is the whole difference between an
   index and a listicle.

It also materially lowers our risk. A published, evidenced, clearly-framed editorial judgement
offered to the subject for comment before publication is a far stronger position than the same
judgement published cold.

---

## Rules for sending

- **One template. Same terms for a AAA and a B.** Softening the letter for low scorers, or
  chasing high scorers for a quote, would make the courtesy a negotiation. Identical treatment
  is the point.
- **Nothing is for sale in this email.** No membership pitch, no audit offer, no "improve your
  score" language, no link to pricing. The moment a rated party can infer that money moves a
  rating, the Index is worthless. If they ask what we sell, answer after publication.
- **Do not negotiate the score in the thread.** Take evidence, thank them, review it against
  the rubric offline. A score moves because the evidence moved, never because the conversation
  was uncomfortable.
- **Never state judgement as fact.** "Our assessment is…" / "we scored this…" — not "your
  campaign had no African authorship". The score is a published editorial judgement against a
  published standard, and the wording must keep saying so.
- **Honour the deadline.** If we promise responses are reviewed and published alongside, that
  has to happen on launch day even if it is inconvenient.
- **Send from a person**, not a no-reply. Replies go to a mailbox that is actually read
  between 16 and 30 September.

## The email

**Subject:** `Ahead of publication: {{brand}} in the Cultural-Signal Index, Edition 01`

---

Dear {{contact_name or "team"}},

I'm writing before we publish, not after.

MonoKromatik runs the **Cultural-Signal Index** — an independent rating of African and diaspora
brand-culture *work*. We rate the work itself: the idea, who originated and controls it, how
well it was made, and what verifiably followed. It is not a brand valuation, not a consumer
survey, and not an awards jury.

**Edition 01 publishes on 30 September 2026, and it includes work associated with {{brand}}.**

Here is exactly what we will publish:

> **{{work_title}}**
> Cultural-Signal Score: **{{score}}/100 — band {{band}}** ({{band_label}})
> {{axis_line}}
> Assessed under rubric {{rubric}} · {{verification}} · {{sources}} sources cited

Our full reasoning for each of the four axes is on the page now: **{{url}}**
The rubric those levels are defined against — including the band cut-offs — is published in
full at **monokromatik.com/intelligence/signal-index/methodology**.

**Two things worth being clear about.**

First, we score a **specific piece of work**, not your company, and not your record. A low
score on one piece is not a verdict on the organisation. Our highest-weighted axis is
authorship — who originated the work and who ends up owning it — which means well-made,
well-funded work can score modestly if the value it created was captured elsewhere. That is
the question this publication exists to ask.

Second, **this is not a sales approach.** There is nothing to buy, and no payment of any kind
can change a score. No rated brand has paid us, and if that ever changes we will disclose it
on the rating itself.

**If we have something wrong, I want to know before 30 September.**

Specifically useful:
- A factual correction, with a source.
- Ownership or contractual detail we could not verify publicly — who held the IP, the masters,
  the licence, the equity.
- Evidence of an outcome: published figures, an audited result, a sustained position. Our
  CONSEQUENCE axis caps at 3 where impact is plausible but unevidenced, so this is the single
  most common reason a score is lower than a brand expects.

Send it to **editor@monokromatik.com** by **{{response_deadline}}**. What happens then:

- Evidence is reviewed against the same published rubric as the original score. If it changes
  the assessment, **the score changes**, and we publish the correction and the reason.
- If it doesn't change the assessment, we say so, and **your response is published alongside
  the rating** if you want it to be.
- If you'd rather not respond, that's fine — nothing is withheld or held over you, and we will
  not characterise silence as agreement.

You're also welcome to tell me the rating is wrong and why, on the record.

Thank you for reading this far.

{{sender_name}}
Editor, MonoKromatik
editor@monokromatik.com · monokromatik.com

## Handling replies

| Reply type | Action |
|---|---|
| **Factual error, evidenced** | Correct within 48h. Publish a dated correction note on the rating. If it moves the score, say so explicitly and show before → after. |
| **New ownership/outcome evidence** | Re-score against the rubric. Movement is the product — a score rising on evidence is a *good* outcome for the Index, not a retreat. |
| **Disagreement, no new evidence** | Score stands. Offer to publish their response verbatim alongside the rating. Reply courteously; do not re-argue. |
| **Request to be removed** | Decline, courteously. The Index rates published work in the public domain. Offer right of reply instead. |
| **Legal threat** | Do not respond in substance. Do not amend under pressure alone — amend only if the evidence warrants it, which is a separate question. Escalate to Sibu; take advice before replying. |
| **Offer of payment / advertising / partnership** | Decline while the rating stands, and disclose the approach if we later publish anything about them. |
| **Press enquiry from the brand's agency** | Route to the standard press pack; don't brief off-record on other brands' scores. |

**Log every reply** — a corrections log published on day +30 is itself a credibility asset and
was on the launch plan for exactly that reason.

## ✅ BLOCKER RESOLVED — 20 July 2026

The four thin-sourced advertisers were re-researched before notification. **Three of the four
were wrong, and one of them was wrong on a fact, not a judgement.** Outcome:

| Brand | Was | Now | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visa** | 54 · AUTH 1 | **68 · AUTH 3** | Our claim was false. Visa commissioned and credited three African illustrators — Karabo Poppy Moletsane (SA), Ahmed El Azazy (Egypt), Wiam Azhar (Morocco) — inside Tap In Studio, plus Kenyan muralist Chelwek Chela. Published on Visa's own site. |
| **Budweiser** | 49 · AUTH 1 | **56 · AUTH 2** | Our claim that the canon was "explicitly non-African" was false — AB InBev's stated eleven-design scope necessarily includes South Africa 2010. Plus commissioned Ivorian artists at the Abidjan festival. |
| **Adidas** | 56 · AUTH 2 | **56 · AUTH 2** | Level holds; reasoning corrected. "Opening-match framing" understated a full SAFA technical partnership, and we had missed Wanda Lephoto and Kabelo Kungwane directing the SA kit launch. |
| **Coca-Cola** | 49 · AUTH 1 | **49 · AUTH 1** | Level holds, now *positively* evidenced: a complete named crew with no African name, and regional authorship funded for the Middle East and India but not Africa. Charge restated as **omission, not extraction**. |

**Works scoring AUTHORSHIP 1 fell from three to one.** The remaining one is the best-evidenced
of the four.

Two traps caught that would have embarrassed us either way:
- The agency credited on Budweiser's hero film is **"Africa Creative" — a São Paulo agency**, not
  an African one. Anyone fact-checking hits it within one search. The piece now names and
  disposes of it.
- **Burna Boy's "Dai Dai" is FIFA's anthem, not Coca-Cola's.** Coca-Cola's is a Van Halen cover.
  Now an explicit `notClaimed`.

**This is the vindication of the pre-notification check.** We had published a universal negative
about three named companies on two sources each, and two of those negatives were falsifiable in
under an hour. Had we sent the notification first, the correction would have arrived as a reply
from a rated brand's comms team — two weeks before launch.

**A rubric weakness to log:** level 1 reads *"African culture referenced or extracted with no
African authorship."* Coca-Cola doesn't extract; it **omits** — African markets are treated as
distribution territory. The rubric has no cell for that, and it is a distinct failure mode worth
naming in v2.1.

## 🔴 ORIGINAL BLOCKER (retained for the record)

Running the pack surfaced a problem that notification would have walked us straight into.

**The four thinnest-sourced works in the sensitive list are the four global advertisers we
rate hardest:**

| Sources | Verification | Band | Score | Brand | AUTHORSHIP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2** | partial | B | 49 | Coca-Cola | **1** |
| **2** | partial | B | 49 | Budweiser (AB InBev) | **1** |
| **2** | partial | BB | 54 | Visa | **1** |
| **2** | partial | BB | 56 | Adidas | 2 |

Median sourcing across all 70 works is **6**. These four sit at 2.

That is the worst available combination: our **lowest scores**, on our **thinnest evidence**,
against the **best-resourced comms and legal teams** in the panel — and they are the recipients
most likely to actually reply. Inviting a right of reply here is inviting a well-funded
challenge to our weakest evidentiary position, in the two weeks before launch.

**There is also a structural problem with the level itself.** AUTHORSHIP 1 reads *"African
culture referenced or extracted with no African authorship at all."* That is a universal
negative. Two sources can establish that we *found* no African authorship; they cannot
establish that there *was* none — an uncredited African director, writer, composer or studio
would falsify it instantly, and on a global campaign that is a live possibility. The rubric's
own rule cuts against us here: *"Evidence or drop a level. If a level can't be justified from
`evidence.confirmed`, it is one level too high."*

**Required before send — one of:**
1. **Deepen sourcing on these four to the corpus median (~6)**, specifically hunting for
   production credits, agency-of-record and any African creative involvement; then re-score
   against what turns up. This is the preferred route and is ~1 day of work.
2. **Or restate the level honestly** — if we cannot evidence the absence of African authorship,
   the defensible score is 2 ("African talent or aesthetics used as *input*") with a note
   saying what we looked for and did not find.

Do not notify these four until one of those is done. Everything else in the pack can proceed.

*(This is a pre-publication fix, not a retreat. The scores may well survive it — but they have
to survive it before we invite scrutiny, not after.)*
