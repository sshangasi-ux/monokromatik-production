This file is the **method of record** for how MonoKromatik reads the financial value of a
culture-driven asset. It exists so our read plugs into an investor's model credibly — and
so, where a corporate-finance partner delivers the classic financial DD, we are an
intelligent, ahead-of-market co-deliverer, not a dependent one.

## The skill areas (what "good" looks like)

1. **Valuation methods, and when each applies.**
   - *DCF* — for cash-generative assets; the discipline is the assumptions, not the model.
   - *Comparables & precedent transactions* — trading and deal multiples; the art is choosing
     a genuinely comparable set (a culture/rights asset rarely has clean public comps).
   - *LBO / returns math* — what a PE buyer can pay and still hit target IRR/MOIC.
   - *Real-options / scenario* — for IP, catalogues and rights whose value is optionality
     under uncertainty (our verticals live here). Pairs with our scenario method.
2. **Intangible & brand valuation** — royalty-relief, excess-earnings, and Brand-Finance-style
   strength-to-value bridges. This is where our Cultural-Signal Index becomes a *financial*
   input: brand strength as a driver of a defensible value, and the strength-vs-value gap as
   a quantified upside (the Springbok read, in numbers).
3. **Quality of earnings & commercial DD** — normalising earnings, revenue durability,
   customer/audience concentration, and — our edge — **value-capture durability**: how much of
   the earnings actually stays with the target vs leaks to a platform, licensor or partner.
4. **The value-capture → valuation bridge (our proprietary layer).** Translate the AOC read
   into value: an EXPORTED or HOLLOWED asset carries a capture discount a naive comp set
   misses; a RETAINED or re-ownable asset carries upside. This is the number no generic
   corp-finance shop produces, and it is the thing to push to maturity 4.
5. **Deal-economics literacy** — sources & uses, earn-outs, minority vs control, sell-on /
   image-rights structures (esp. in sport/music), so our post-deal retention plan is
   structurable, not just strategic.

### 2026-09-23 — Sweep 01: valuation method & "quality of capture"

**Focus:** how to put a defensible number on a culture-driven asset, and where our
value-capture read plugs into an investor's model. Researched against IVS 210,
intangible/brand valuation practice, music-catalogue valuation, and PE quality-of-earnings
/ commercial-DD method.

**1. Intangible & brand valuation — the three approaches, and when each applies.**
- *Income — Relief-from-Royalty (RfR):* value = PV of the royalties you'd avoid by owning
  (vs licensing) the mark. The standard method for **trademarks / brands / trade names**.
  This is our **strength-to-value bridge**: the Cultural-Signal Index brand-strength score is
  what justifies the *royalty rate* — a stronger, harder-to-fake signal commands a higher
  defensible rate — so the Index becomes a *financial* input, not just an editorial one.
- *Income — Multi-Period Excess Earnings (MPEEM):* isolates the cash flow attributable to the
  *primary* intangible after deducting **contributory-asset charges** (returns on the other
  assets that help generate it). Per IVS 210, apply MPEEM to a **single** primary intangible
  per income stream; contributory intangibles valued by RfR carry a charge equal to their
  (after-tax) royalty. Use it for the asset that actually drives the earnings — a catalogue, a
  flagship brand, the audience relationship.
- *Market:* comparable royalty rates / licensing deals (RfR inputs) and transaction multiples.
- *Cost:* replacement / reproduction — a floor, rarely the driver for a culture asset.

**2. Rights & catalogue valuation (music, broadcast, image rights).** Practice is
**income + market**, not real-options — real-options is discussed academically but is *not*
market-standard, so never lead a quote with it. Use DCF on projected royalties off 5–10 years
of history, and an **LTM-revenue multiple** benchmarked to recent comparable transactions.
Interrogate the multiple drivers: remaining copyright/rights life, catalogue "standards"
(durable earners), earnings stability/decay, and *new avenues of exploitation* — exactly where
our signalling read finds mispriced upside (a high-signal, under-exploited catalogue).

**3. Quality of Earnings (QoE) vs commercial DD — and our extension.**
- *QoE* is backward-looking: confirms revenue is **real, correctly recognised, recurring**.
  *Commercial DD* is forward-looking: **can they deliver it again** at scale and margin. Both
  are table stakes an investor already buys.
- **Revenue durability** is tested by **cohort retention and contract type**, not blended
  churn; **customer / audience concentration** can cut the multiple by **1–2×**.
- **Our proprietary extension — "Quality of Capture":** QoE asks whether the earnings are
  real; we ask **how much of the real earnings actually stays with the target vs leaks** to a
  platform, licensor, agency or offshore parent. It is the AOC read expressed as a
  QoE-adjacent metric — the number no generic QoE or commercial-DD provider produces.

**4. The capture-discount frame — now with a method (our edge).**
1. Derive a *naive* value the standard way (RfR / MPEEM / comps).
2. Estimate the **capture rate** — the share of the asset's economics the target actually
   keeps (from the AOC read: EXPORTED / HOLLOWED = low, RETAINED = high).
3. Apply a **capture haircut** to the naive value for the leaked share, and quantify a
   **re-ownership premium** for the portion recoverable by owning the certification apparatus
   (the delta between current and achievable capture).
4. The spread between the haircut value and the re-ownership value **is the deal's
   value-creation thesis** — priced, not asserted.

**Sources (this sweep):** IVSC IVS 210 (Intangible Assets); MPI, PCE, Eton Venture Services,
Opagio on RfR / MPEEM / the approaches; Royalty Exchange and Citrin Cooperman on
music-catalogue valuation; Blue Ridge Partners and Growth Shuttle on quality-of-revenue vs
QoE, cohort/contract durability and concentration. Method references, verified this run;
figures in a live engagement are always sourced to the target.

### 2026-09-24 — Sweep 03: comps library v0, the country-risk layer, and pricing control

**Focus:** the two "to reach 3" items from Sweep 01 — a first **comps library** for African /
culture-asset transactions, and making the capture-discount frame survive an investor's
cost-of-capital scrutiny. Researched against Damodaran's country-risk data, Brand Finance's
royalty-relief method, music-catalogue multiple reporting, and reported African culture deals.

**1. The country-risk layer — and the rule that keeps our frame honest.**
Damodaran's country table (5 Jan 2026 data) prices African equity risk on top of a **4.23%**
mature-market ERP:

| Market | Rating | Country risk premium | Total ERP |
|---|---|---|---|
| South Africa | Ba2 | 3.90% | 8.13% |
| Nigeria | B3 | 8.41% | 12.64% |
| Kenya / Ghana / Egypt | Caa1 | 9.71% | 13.94% |

*(A July 2026 update exists — SA reported at 8.46% — always pull the live table on an
engagement; these are method anchors, not engagement inputs.)*
- **The no-double-count rule (our method):** the **capture haircut lives in the cash flows**;
  **country risk lives in the discount rate**. A leak to an offshore platform is a *certain*
  loss of economics, not a risk — so it reduces the numerator. Never also inflate the discount
  rate "because it's Africa" to express the same leak. An investor who sees both will
  (rightly) discount our whole read. Stating this separation explicitly is itself a
  credibility signal.
- **Exposure, not domicile:** apply country risk by where the *revenue* is earned (Damodaran's
  operations-weighted approach), not where the entity is incorporated. A Lagos-authored asset
  earning mostly in USD streaming royalties carries less Nigerian CRP than its address implies
  — and a HOLLOWED asset (local licence, offshore owner) may carry local risk on local cash
  flows while the upside is booked offshore. That asymmetry is a finding in itself.

**2. Relief-from-royalty, made ours — the Index → royalty-rate bridge.**
Brand Finance's method (ISO 10668-compliant; the firm states RfR is used in over 80% of brand
valuations) runs seven steps: Brand Strength Index (0–100) → sector royalty *range* from
comparable licences → BSI positions the rate inside the range (their example: BSI 80 in a
0–5% sector ⇒ 4%) → brand-attributable revenue → forecast → royalty stream → post-tax PV.
**Our adaptation (two changes, both defensible):**
1. The **Cultural-Signal Index** positions the rate inside the sector range — same mechanics,
   but our strength score is built on *hard-to-fake signals* (certification, authorship,
   scarcity), which is exactly what a licensee pays for.
2. The royalty is applied only to **captured** revenue — the brand-attributable revenue that
   actually reaches the owner after the AOC leak. RfR on gross revenue over-values an EXPORTED
   brand; RfR on captured revenue is the capture-discounted brand value, and the delta to
   gross is the **re-ownership premium** in Sweep 01's frame. One method, two numbers, and the
   spread is the thesis.

**4. Pricing control separately from economics — the Springbok lesson.**
The ASG proposal paired **20% of the economics** with **three of seven board seats and the
right to appoint the chair** of the commercial-rights company. That is a minority price for a
disproportionate share of the *certification apparatus* — who decides how the brand is
licensed, sponsored and exploited. The member unions' rejection (7 of 13 against; 75% needed)
is, in our language, the market refusing a control-for-economics trade at a minority price.
**Method:** in any culture-asset deal, value the **economic stake** and the **governance/control
rights** as two lines — a control premium (or, for the seller, a *control-transfer discount*
they are accepting) — and map each control right to the AOC asset it governs. This is where our
valuation and legal files meet (see `legal-and-transaction.md`, Sweep 03, sport checklist).

**Emerging to watch:** catalogue multiples' sensitivity to rates (the same stable stream is
worth "several turns less" at a higher discount rate — Billboard Italia/Signal, Shot Tower);
institutional royalty vehicles (ABS/funds) making music a benchmarked asset class
(Neuberger Berman) — which will make *un-benchmarked* African catalogues look cheap to them,
and makes a defensible capture read our wedge.
